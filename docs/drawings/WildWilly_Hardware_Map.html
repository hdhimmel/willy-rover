⚠ This is the §4.7 target, not the rover on the bench tonight. The two Pico links, the encoders leaving I²C and the sonars leaving the Pi header are all design, nothing fitted. As built today: an MCP23017 at 0x27 reads the encoders over I²C, the three sonars land directly on the Pi header (GP4/5/13/14/21/26), the drive motors are the 620 RPM 17.1:1 set, and the bus carries eleven devices. Where this map and that differ, the difference is §4.7.

Who owns what, and why it sits there

OwnerJobWhy not somewhere else
Pi 5AI, navigation, vision, voice, the FSM and the safety gateEverything with variable latency lives here. Nothing in the reflex path may depend on it finishing on time
Pico ASix quadrature encoders, PIO decode; R5 rail sense on ADC24,363 counts/s per wheel is far past what I²C polling or a MicroPython interrupt can follow. It shares R5 with the encoders on purpose — when that rail sags the encoders go static and A stays alive to say so
Pico BThree HC-SR04 timings; the BNO085 reset lineEcho timing is microseconds. Fed from Pi 5 V, not the sonar rail — R2 dies with the base 12 V, so a B on R2 would go silent exactly when it should be reporting that its sensors lost power

R5 is the rail with no monitor and a history. Its branch P8 has no recorded fuse and no recorded gauge, and the encoders have already been lost once to an unmonitored 3.3 V supply. That is the entire argument for putting Pico A on it.
