Pico Carrier Schematic

A single carrier design for both Pico 2 W boards. Same layout, same footprints, stuffed differently: the divider is fitted on A, the reset pull-up on B. Everything else is common, so a dead Pico is a swap rather than a rewire.

What goes on each board

No external status LED — dropped 2026-09-26. The Pico 2 W has one on the CYW43439, driven as Pin("LED") in MicroPython. Earlier revisions here claimed there was no usable onboard LED; that was wrong. The cost is that driving it powers up the wireless chip and loads its firmware over SPI — no network, no transmission, but §12 item 17, "do not initialise the radios", needs amending to permit it. Dropping R1 and LED1 also frees GP14 and removes the one hazard that could not be designed out: a bare GP14 lead crossing the GP15 reset net on Board B.

Stuffing the 30-column board

⛔ REV A–F HAD THE PICO THE WRONG WAY ROUND. Owner-caught 2026-09-26; boards built to those revisions cannot be rescued by re-seating the Pico. The pins point down and the board is seen from above, so laying it with the USB at the left puts pins 21–40 along the TOP row and pins 1–20 along the BOTTOM — the top-left pin is pin 40, VBUS. Earlier revisions had it inverted, which put every signal on the power side and every power connection on the signal side. No rotation fixes it: a 180° turn maps the top row to pin 21−column, not column, so no orientation makes the old holes right. Every part is reusable — F1, D1, R2, R3, R4 and the sockets desolder and go straight onto the new board. You need two fresh 30-column boards.

30 columns and bus rails changes the design, and simplifies it. The Pico needs 20 columns, so it sits on the board instead of beside it — no separate mounting, and nothing on these drawings needs a wire between board and Pico. The bus rails carry the feed and the ground, so most of the jumpers disappear too. Rows A–E and F–J are 5-hole tie-strips split by the centre channel; each bus line runs the length of the board.

The Pico sits on columns 11–30, not 1–20 — owner-directed 2026-09-26. Shifting it right frees the ten columns to its left for F1 and D1, which puts the protection chain immediately beside the pin it protects: and then the jumpers go away entirely (owner, same day): F1 stands from the + rail straight into column 9, and D1 spans column 9 to column 12 — which is pin 39, VSYS. The 24-column link across the board and the jumper that fed it are both gone; the parts do the connecting. The USB plug reaches back over columns 5–10 at rows D–G — mid-height, so it passes under nothing: F1 and D1 live in rows A and B, above it. Keep the cable from draping on them.

Put the Pico's pins in rows C and H. Its rows are 17.78 mm / 0.700″ apart, which is exactly C to H — and that leaves rows A, B reachable for pins 21–40 and rows I, J reachable for pins 1–20. Land it in A and F instead and the body covers B through E, taking every access hole on that side with it. Pin 1 and pin 40 share column 11 — both are at the USB end. ⚠ Whether it clears the board edge depends on your board's margin, which I cannot know. The plug now sits over the board by design, reaching back about five columns at mid-height. Check it clears F1 and D1 in rows A/B, and that the Pico's far end at column 30 does not overhang your right margin awkwardly. Column c then holds pin 51−c on the row-C side (top) and pin c−10 on the row-H side (bottom) — so every power connection is in the top section and every signal in the bottom.

Rail order, corrected twice by the owner. The top pair is reversed relative to the bottom, so reading down the board the four rails are − + … + − — both + rails are the inner ones. That is a gift for this layout: F1 and R2 stand from the + rail straight into row A without crossing anything, which is what makes the jumperless chain safe.

Bus assignment. Upper + (inner) = raw V+ in — F1 stands directly in it, and on A so does R2. Upper − (outer) = ground: the rover's GND lands here and pin 38 returns to it. Lower + (inner) = 3V3 in, used on B only for R4; unused on A. Lower − (outer) carries no component, but tie it to the upper − rail at column 30: on B the 3V3 and the 5V arrive from the same Pi and must share one ground, and a single tie is what makes that true on the board rather than only at the Pi. ⚠ Rails on many of these boards are split at the midpoint — check yours is continuous, or bridge the break, or the right-hand half is dead.

⚠ Two clearances, and that is the whole list. Column 11, rows A/B: that strip is pin 40, VBUS, and D1’s body floats over it. It is empty by rule anyway, which is exactly what makes the span safe — but never put anything there. The pin-38 ground wire passes the upper + rail at column 13 on its way out to −, which is why it is drawn with a jog; that + rail hole at column 13 must stay empty. Reversing the top pair removed the other two clearances this layout used to need.

Where the device wires land

Every device wire lands in row I. That is forced, not chosen: the Pico body covers rows D–G of columns 11–30, so the only holes sharing a strip with a pin are A, B (row-C side, pins 21–40 — power) and I, J (row-H side, pins 1–20 — every GPIO this design uses). Land the wire in row I and keep row J as the spare for metering.

⚠ This is also the trap. Every column 11–30 has a Pico pin on both sides, so a hole that looks free may not be. Before landing anything in rows A/B, work out 51 − column and check what that pin is. Two on that side bite hardest: column 15 is pin 36, 3V3 OUT — landing the raw feed there would tie the unregulated rail to the Pico's own regulator output, which an earlier revision of this sheet did — and column 11 is pin 40, VBUS: leave it empty.

Spare pins, and where to put an expansion header

The top section from column 19 rightwards is nearly empty, and that is where the spare GPIOs are. Pins 21–40 land on that side, and everything this design needs from it sits in columns 12, 13 and — on A — 17 and 18. So columns 19–30 of rows A/B are free on both boards, and they carry GP16–GP22, GP26 and GP27: both SPI0 sets, both I²C sets, and two more ADC channels. A 1×8 or 2×6 header across rows A/B of columns 19–30 picks up most of it without disturbing anything.

⚠ Four holes on the top side are not spare GPIOs, whatever the grid suggests. Column 11 = pin 40 VBUS — leave empty. Column 14 = pin 37 3V3_EN: pull it low and the Pico's regulator shuts down. Column 16 = pin 35 ADC_VREF — leave as fitted; it is the reference the R5 divider is measured against. Column 21 = pin 30 RUN — pulling it low resets the chip. And on A, columns 12, 13, 17, 18 are the power chain and the divider.

One caution before you load these up. The 170 RPM motor swap and §4.7 both still have open items, and every pin you commit now is one the reflex layer cannot have later. GP26/GP27 in particular are the only spare ADC inputs, and §14 item 17 wants an ADS1115 channel or a Pico ADC for charge sense.

Build notes

  1. One wire on the whole board: pin 38 to ground — column 13, top section, jogged past the + rail on its way out to −. Board A only: its Pi signal ground lands separately at pin 18, column 28, because A's supply return goes to R5 — a different source, and each source needs its own return. Every GND pin on the Pico is internally common, so those two are enough. Board B has no column-28 landing: both of its supplies come from the same Pi, so one ground wire does both jobs and a second would close a loop through the Pico. AGND, pin 33 at column 18, is tied to neither rail: R3's far end is its only connection, which single-points the divider's return at the Pico.
  2. No bulk capacitor on VSYS, deliberately. The Pico carries its own, and adding more raises inrush through F1 — the one thing that would make a healthy rover nuisance-trip a fuse and hand you a silently dead board.
  3. Check the orientation before the sockets go down. USB at the column-11 end, and the top-left Pico pin — column 11, row C — must read VBUS. If it reads GP0 the board is round the wrong way and every hole on this sheet is wrong — that is exactly what happened to the first pair. Then build both in one sitting and mark them before stuffing: they are identical until R2/R3 or R4 go in. Write A or B on the copper, not on tape.
  4. Keep the Pico’s short edge clear — nothing overhangs the micro-USB port. Programming is USB only: BOOTSEL, cable, drag the .uf2. None of J1–J4 is involved and the REPL runs over USB CDC, not over J2. Flash both boards bare before they go in the sockets; afterwards they can be reflashed in place, and D1 is what makes that safe — USB 5 V reaches VSYS through the Pico’s internal Schottky but cannot get back out onto the rover rail. §12 item 19 predates that diode, so unplugging the rover feed first is now belt-and-braces rather than the only protection. BOOTSEL faces up in the sockets and needs no cutout.
  5. Populate J1 last. Every check below is done with no power connected; fitting the input connector last makes that hard to get wrong.
  6. Two pin assignments moved off the §4.7 sketch, because one shared layout forbids them. (a) Both Picos now run UART0 on GP12/GP13, pins 16/17. The sketch had B on GP0/GP1, which cannot work here — J2 would have to land on pins 1/2 and 16/17 at once. (b) B’s BNO085 RST moves from GP10 to GP15, pin 20. GP10 is LR Phase A on board A, so with one J3 footprint fitted to both boards, way 11 would have sat on B’s reset net — a live stub on an active-low reset, and a dead short onto it the first time anything was plugged into a 12-way housing on the wrong board. The payoff beyond avoiding those: B’s sonar now occupies J3 ways 1–6 contiguously, so the sonar harness is a plain 6-way into the first six positions.
  7. B’s feed is the Pi’s 5 V, taken at the breakout terminal — never R2. R2 dies with the base 12 V, so a Pico B on it goes dark exactly when it should be reporting that its sensor rail is down; and ~40 mA on R2 blurs the 15 mA-per-sonar signal that INA260 0x40 uses to catch a shorted HC-SR04. The display already has one of header pins 2/4 and which one is unrecorded, so the breakout terminal avoids the question. A’s feed is the encoder 3V3 distribution — meter its polarity first, it was found reversed on 2026-09-18.
Four meter checks before a Pico ever sits in the sockets. 1. J1-1 to VSYS socket pin: forward a few hundred mV through D1, open the other way. Backwards diode is the fault that only shows up as "board won't boot". 2. J1-1 to J1-2: open. Any reading is a short across the rover rail. 3. F1 cold resistance: under ~0.5Ω. Several ohms and it will eat Pico A's 3.3 V feed. 4. On A only — R2 + R3 end to end ≈ 20k, and the TP2 node ≈ half of it to each end. That ratio is what ADC2 reports as the rail voltage.
